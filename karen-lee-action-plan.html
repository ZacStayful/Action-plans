What drives demand around Middleton Cheney

🏁
Silverstone & the British Grand Prix

Around 20–25 minutes away. Grand Prix weekend in July is the single biggest rate spike in the year, and the circuit runs events through the season. Groups travelling together want a whole house — your dining table for ten is a genuine selling point.

🏎️
Motorsport Valley

You're surrounded by motorsport engineering — Mercedes' F1 base is in Brackley and Aston Martin's is at Silverstone, with a large supplier network around them. That means visiting engineers, contractors and teams on multi-night stays outside race weekends.

🚧
HS2 construction

The HS2 works run through the countryside around Banbury and Brackley. Construction crews need a base for weeks or months at a time — exactly the longer-stay, repeat-booking guest who can move to direct bookings.

🛣️
Banbury, the M40 & the rail line

Two minutes from the M40 at Banbury, with Banbury station on the Chiltern line to London Marylebone and Birmingham. Business travellers and contractors working across Banbury's industrial estates.

🌳
Cotswolds, Oxford & Stratford

A village base with pubs, a Co-op and takeaways on the doorstep, within easy reach of the Cotswolds, Oxford and Stratford-upon-Avon. Families and friends' groups at weekends and in school holidays.

🏥
Horton General Hospital

Around 5 km away in Banbury. Visiting medical staff and families of patients — steady, low-key demand in the quieter months.

A correction from the call. You were right to be surprised — the demand section I showed you didn't pick up Silverstone or the F1 teams at all, and listed a festival 16 km away as the main event driver instead. The cards above are the accurate picture for your location.

Your questions, answered honestly

Because the £65,328 gross is everything a guest pays, including the cleaning fee. The cleaning fee comes in and goes straight back out to the cleaner — a pass-through, exactly as you put it. It never comes out of your accommodation income.
A little, sometimes — a direct guest might get a slightly better rate than on Airbnb. But there's no 15% platform commission on a direct booking, and longer stays mean fewer changeovers, so you keep more of every booking overall. The guest gets a small saving and the convenience of dealing with one contact; you get a more profitable booking.
It's a genuine trade-off, and a good spot. That's why we build reviews first with shorter stays in the opening months, then bring in HS2 and motorsport contractors once the listing is established. Long stays then become the quiet-season backbone rather than something that stalls the review count.
We track the listings we're competing against and where yours ranks. Pricing software proposes rates weekly — often daily — based on what the market is doing, and we approve or override them. We don't leave it fully automated: a characterful new conversion will often deserve more than the software suggests, because the software can't see what the property looks like. The goal is the top 10% of the local market, where you can command the best rates and stay busy.
Management is 15% + VAT. There's also a £42 monthly software fee deducted from payouts (it's in the agreement, and I should have mentioned it on the call). No onboarding fee. The agreement has a six-month initial term — leaving within that period carries a £1,000 early-exit fee — then it rolls on three months' notice, so guests who have already booked are honoured. Jobs up to £300 are approved without chasing you.
The honest version: Year 1 will be lower while reviews build, which is why we plan on £27,178, not £33,972. If every month performed like January (£1,578), the year would come to about £18,900 — still above the £15,120 single let before bills, but once you pay the bills yourself the two would be roughly level. That's the floor, not the forecast: the local market is thin on reviews (average 44), which is what makes the full figures realistic within 5–8 months. And you're never locked in beyond the six-month term.
Both work. Going live in time for Christmas means earning through December and arriving at January — the quietest month — with some reviews already in place. If family are coming over Christmas, block that week in the calendar (it costs roughly £480 of Year 1 income) and still go live either side of it. Starting in January means the listing launches in the slowest month with zero reviews, which slows the ramp slightly. Either way, the aim is to be well established by July for the Grand Prix.
One more correction to the material you printed: the compliance page described safety checks as optional outside London and Scotland. That's not right for England. Paying guests bring fire safety law into play, so you'll need a fire risk assessment, smoke and carbon monoxide alarms, a fire blanket and suitable escape routes; a gas safety certificate if there's any gas; and an electrical check (EICR) is strongly recommended. You'll also want holiday-let insurance. Because the schoolroom sits within your own grounds, check the planning decision notice for any condition tying its use to the main house — if there is one, it's worth confirming with West Northamptonshire Council before you list. We'll help with all of this during setup.
In England a self-catering let moves onto business rates only if it's available to let for at least 140 days a year and actually let for at least 70. Until it meets those thresholds it stays on council tax. Once it qualifies, small business rate relief can reduce the bill significantly. We can talk this through once the listing has a booking history.
We'd let it as a whole house, not by the room. Individual rooms compete directly with hotels, so you end up undercutting them in a constant price war. A whole house offers something hotels can't — a group sharing the cost. If the living space doesn't suit six, we'd list it as a five-bed and turn the spare room into a games room or similar. Send over the link and I'll run the figures ahead of the students leaving in June 2027.
