Your questions, answered honestly

Q: What's the minimum we could realistically achieve?
A: Worst-case is around £1,100/month (your August figure) — but that's before the review-velocity fixes below take effect. Your likely range once we're active is £1,700–1,800/month average, with upside once the listing is established.
Q: Do you think this is totally achievable?
A: Yes — but it's not automatic, and I don't want to pretend it is. It's achievable if the reviews get chased properly and the pricing isn't run ahead of the listing's credibility. That's the part your last two management companies seem to have missed.
Q: Why aren't we getting bookings — June was worst month despite good reviews?
A: Most likely the listing was priced too ambitiously for its current review count during peak season — guests will pay a premium for an established listing, but not yet for yours. We'll want to see your actual Airbnb/Booking.com link to confirm this and check for any listing gaps.
Q: Is short-let even still viable here, or should we go long-term let?
A: Short-let is viable — the property, location and demand all support it. The issue has been listing management, not the property or the strategy. Even during a slower ramp-up period, short-let nets meaningfully more than long-let on this flat.
Q: What's the honest worst case if these projections aren't hit?
A: If review velocity doesn't pick up, the ramp period could run longer than the ~13 months discussed, and you could sit closer to the £1,100–1,400/month band for an extended stretch. That's the real risk — not the property, the market, or the platform, but how proactively the reviews and pricing are managed month to month. It's exactly why we track review pace as closely as we do.
