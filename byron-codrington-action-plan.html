What drives bookings around Hickling Pastures

Only drivers we're confident genuinely apply to your location.

🏙️
Nottingham — events, sport & universities

The city is a short drive west. Trent Bridge cricket, concerts and theatre, and two large universities (open days, graduations, visiting family) all send people looking for space outside the centre — especially groups and families who want a house rather than a hotel room.

🥧
Melton Mowbray

The market town down the A606 is known for its pork pies and Stilton and markets itself as a food destination, with food festivals and a busy livestock and market scene that bring weekend visitors.

🏰
Vale of Belvoir & countryside breaks

Rolling countryside, village pubs, walking and cycling, with Belvoir Castle a scenic drive away. This is exactly the audience your comparables serve — 3-bed rural homes at a £351 average nightly rate.

East Midlands connectivity

Easy reach of Loughborough, Leicester, the A46 and East Midlands Airport — useful for contractors, business visitors and families visiting relatives across the region, which fills the quieter midweek nights.

Your questions, answered straight

Fair challenge, and I didn't give you the full picture on the call. From every £100 a guest pays: about £15 goes in platform commission, about £18 is the cleaning and laundry fee (the guest pays it, the cleaner receives it), and our management fee is 15% plus VAT (£18). On top, there's a £42/month software fee. That's why you'd seen "30–40%" quoted — once cleaning is included, roughly half of what the guest pays reaches you. When comparing providers, ask each one for the net payout on the same booking, including VAT, software and cleaning — headline percentages alone aren't comparable.
Neither — I got this wrong on the call. It's a separate monthly software fee (our channel manager, pricing and messaging tools), deducted from your payout. It's charged every month the agreement is live, including your summer months, so it's already included in every net figure in this plan.
The guest pays it as a separate line on their booking. It appears on your monthly statement and goes to the cleaner for the turnover. It isn't taken from your nightly income — but it is part of the gross figure, which is why we show it as a deduction to get from gross to net.
They come from 12 active short-let listings within about 8 km — what similar homes actually earn — not a guess. They don't yet reflect your upgrades, because we haven't seen the house. That can cut both ways: a well-finished home can command the top of the local range (the best local listing earns ~£119k gross with a hot tub), but condition, layout and parking matter too. Your photos will let me confirm or adjust the numbers honestly.
You have live access to the booking calendar and we block your months at setup, so no guest can book them. You can change dates — ideally with plenty of notice, because confirmed bookings are honoured. If you ever want to release part of September (a shoulder month worth ~£2,600 net), just tell us.
Year 1 is the risk year. With no reviews, the first few months can run 10–20% below forecast, and the months you're letting include the quieter October–February stretch. If every let month performed like October at Year 1 rates, the house would net around £11,700 over the eight months — well short of the mortgage. That's extremely unlikely because it assumes January–May perform like the quietest month of the year, but you should plan with a buffer for Year 1 rather than relying on the headline number.
Three things worth checking before you commit to any provider — we're not tax or mortgage advisers, so please confirm with professionals: (1) Your mortgage lender's consent — most residential mortgages require permission (or a switch) for short-term letting. (2) Insurance — standard home insurance won't cover paying guests; you'll need holiday-let / short-let cover, which the agreement requires. (3) Tax — as non-UK residents you'll come under HMRC's Non-Resident Landlord Scheme, and the US taxes worldwide income, so a UK–US cross-border accountant is worth an hour of your time.
An initial six-month term (early exit in that period carries a £1,000 fee), then a rolling agreement with three months' written notice either side. The listing is managed on your property, not locked away from you — and the property stays yours to use in your blocked months.
