What drives demand around Plas Newton Lane

🏰
Chester's historic centre

The most complete Roman and medieval city walls in Britain, the Rows and the Cathedral make Chester one of the North West's biggest short-break destinations — weekend demand runs all year, not just in summer.

🦁
Chester Zoo

One of the UK's most-visited zoos sits in Upton, a short drive north of Plas Newton Lane. Families want a whole house with a garden and parking rather than a hotel room — exactly what your cottage offers.

🏇
Chester Racecourse

The Roodee is the oldest racecourse still in use. Race days from May through September — especially the May Festival — fill the city on peak-rate weekends, which is part of why May is one of your strongest months.

🏥
Countess of Chester Hospital

About 1.3km away. Visiting families, locum and agency clinicians and people attending appointments need somewhere close for days or weeks at a time — steady midweek demand that tourism doesn't give you.

🚆
Rail links

Bache station (Merseyrail, direct to Liverpool) is under a kilometre away, and Chester station has direct trains to London, Manchester and North Wales — easy for weekenders and business visitors arriving without a car.

🏭
Contractors & business visitors

Airbus's wing factory at Broughton, just over the Welsh border, plus the University of Chester and city businesses bring contractors, visiting staff and graduation-week families who book whole homes for multi-night stays.

This is the one thing I need to correct from the call. I said a standard buy-to-let would be fine for short-letting as long as that's the main use. That's not right — most standard buy-to-let mortgages don't allow holiday or short lets. For this you'd normally need a holiday-let mortgage, or written consent from your lender. You also need your current lender's consent before any letting at all, short or long. Holiday-let products can be interest-only, which lowers the monthly payment, but the rates are usually higher than residential ones and the capital isn't being paid off — so the '£300 a month saving' I mentioned was a rough guess, not a quote. As your deal ends next year, the right move is a broker quote for a holiday-let mortgage specifically. Every figure in this plan assumes your mortgage stays at £700.

In Chester, you don't need a licence or planning permission to short-let a whole house like yours in the normal case, and a neighbour can't stop you letting your own property. Two things to know. First, England's national short-let register is coming — the government has said it expects it to be fully running by March 2027, which is likely before you go live. It's a registration, not a licence: you'll register the property and show your safety paperwork (fire risk assessment, gas safety, electrical check). Second, a difficult neighbour can complain to the council about noise or anti-social behaviour — that's the real risk, so guest screening matters more for you than for most owners. Keeping your garden studio fenced off and locked, as you planned, also helps.

Once the cottage isn't your main home, its council tax treatment changes. Councils can charge a premium on furnished second homes, but a property that's available to let for 140+ nights a year and actually let for 70+ can move to business rates instead — and small properties can often qualify for small business rate relief. Worth checking with Cheshire West and Chester Council closer to the time; I'll help you with the application once there's a letting history.

I'm not a tax adviser, so please take proper cross-border advice before you move. What I can tell you factually: UK rental income stays taxable in the UK when you're non-resident, and HMRC's Non-Resident Landlord Scheme is likely to apply — you can apply to receive your payouts without basic-rate tax deducted. Spain may also tax it, usually with credit for UK tax paid.
