What drives demand around B33 9QB

With 1,954 events a year, the NEC is the single biggest booking engine in this postcode. Exhibitions and conferences bring exhibitors, contractors and crew who need multi-night stays close by and book early. It is the reason almost every competing listing near you puts "NEC" in its title.

🚄Lea Hall station — 0.47 km

Ten transport links sit within reach, with Lea Hall station a five-minute walk. That puts Birmingham city centre and Birmingham International within an easy hop, which widens your guest pool well beyond people with cars.

🎓Solihull College, Woodlands Campus — 3.82 km

Colleges generate a steady trickle of relocation, assessor and visiting-staff stays that are largely immune to the summer holiday cycle. It is not the biggest driver here, but it helps hold up the shoulder and low months.

Your questions, answered straight

I want to price the furnishing up myself — how does your quote actually compare?+

Completely fair, and we would rather you checked. Our full quote for the flat is £5,480. The itemised version is coming across separately, but the shape of it is: soft furnishings £900, two double beds £700, sofabed £750, kitchen pack £350, electrical pack £330, TV and stand £350, paint £300, dining table and chairs £250, professional photography £250, labour £600, plus keysafes, side tables and clothes racks.

Most of the physical items are Dunelm, Argos, B&Q and Amazon. If you have the time and a van, you will beat us on materials — we are not going to pretend otherwise. What our number buys you is the labour to fit it out in a day rather than over several weekends, the professional photography, and a spec built from what actually converts in this comp set rather than guesswork. Send us your list and we will tell you honestly which lines are worth doing yourself.

One thing worth knowing: the property analysis you have also carries a leaner setup estimate of around £3,110. That version excludes beds, the sofabed, storage and the dining set. The £5,480 figure is the complete, ready-to-let spec, which is why it is higher.

Why does year one come in lower than the headline figures?+

Because a brand-new listing has no reviews and no ranking history, and both of those drive where you appear in search. It takes roughly twelve months to compete on equal footing with the established listings around you.

So we plan on year one at 80% of the full run rate — £14,062 net rather than £17,577. That is the number we would rather you budget against. If it lands better, good, but we would rather set the expectation low and beat it. Even at that reduced level you are ahead of the £9,169 the long-let nets you, in every single month of the year.

What is the honest worst case if the projections are not hit?+

The honest worst case is that the listing underperforms the comp set — slower to get reviews, weaker ranking, softer nightly rate — and year one comes in below £14,062. The long-let is the safer, more predictable number: £764 a month, arriving whether or not anyone books.

There are two things that make this a smaller risk here than in most areas. The comp set is thin (12 listings) and the direct-booking score is the maximum 100/100. But this is a projection, not a guarantee, and there is no guaranteed-rent floor underneath it. You also have a real cost at risk: the £5,480 setup. If short-let did not work out, you would have a furnished flat — which lets more easily and at a higher rent as a furnished long-let — but you would not get that cash back on day one.

The mortgage is around £350 a month. Even the weakest month in the year-one forecast, January at £828 net, covers it comfortably.

How long am I tied in, and what does it cost to get out?+

Six months initially, then it rolls with three months' written notice from either side. There is no onboarding or setup fee for the management itself.

If you pull the property out during the first six months there is a £1,000 early exit fee, which covers the onboarding work and lost forward bookings. After the initial term it is simply the three months' notice. It is all in the management agreement, and there is a video walkthrough of it above — worth ten minutes before you sign anything.

Are there any short-let restrictions I need to worry about?+

Not for this property. Birmingham has no 90-day cap or short-let licensing regime, so Crossfield Road can be let year-round without a planning issue.

Your Enfield property is a different matter, and I want to flag it properly. Enfield is a London borough, and the whole of Greater London is covered by the 90-day rule — you cannot let an entire dwelling on short lets for more than 90 nights in a calendar year without applying for planning permission for change of use. That changes the maths on Enfield significantly compared with Birmingham. When you are ready to look at it, we should run it as its own conversation rather than assume it works the same way.

What is the management fee, and does the multi-property rate apply to me?+

Standard is 15% plus VAT of gross rental revenue. At three or more properties that drops to 12% plus VAT.

With Crossfield Road on its own you are on 15%. Enfield would take you to two. The 12% rate is a standing volume rate rather than a time-limited offer, so there is no deadline pressure on it — it applies whenever you reach three properties. There is also a £42 monthly software fee, which is already deducted in the net figures shown here.

One thing to carry forward on Enfield. On our call we covered that Birmingham has no short-let restrictions, which is right. But Enfield is a London borough and the 90-day rule does apply there. Before you assume the Crossfield Road numbers translate, let's look at Enfield on its own terms.
