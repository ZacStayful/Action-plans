What drives demand around Mulberry Avenue
Correction from our call: the "who books" section I showed you pulled data for Manchester, not Leicester. That was wrong — here is the accurate picture for your area.
🏥
Glenfield Hospital

You're on the Glenfield border, close to Glenfield Hospital — one of Leicester's three main hospitals and a regional heart and lung centre. Families of patients, visiting clinicians and agency staff need somewhere to stay for several nights, often at short notice.

🛣️
M1 / M69 at Junction 21

The west side of Leicester sits close to Junction 21, where the M1 and M69 meet. That brings contractors and engineering crews working on projects across the region, who want a whole house with parking for a team rather than several hotel rooms.

🛍️
Fosse Park & Meridian

Fosse Park retail park and the business parks around Meridian and Grove Park are nearby. Local listings advertise Fosse Park in their titles for a reason — it's a recognisable pull for visiting families and business travellers.

🎓
Two universities

The University of Leicester and De Montfort University bring a steady flow of visiting parents, open days and graduations every year — reliable short stays for a family-sized house.

⚽
Sport and city events

Leicester City football, Leicester Tigers rugby and the city's festivals (including one of the largest Diwali celebrations outside India) create spikes of demand on match days and event weekends.

👨‍👩‍👧
Visiting friends and family

Leicester is a large, diverse city. Family get-togethers, weddings and celebrations need a whole home for a group — that's exactly what a furnished 3-bed with parking offers.

Your questions, answered honestly
It's a fair question and the answer is in the comparables: 12 active Airbnb listings within roughly 1.7 miles of you, several 3-bed houses earning £19k–£25k gross a year. Your bookings won't come from tourists alone — they come from people visiting Glenfield Hospital, contractors working around Junction 21, families visiting the universities, and visitors to the city for football, events and family. Your projection sits in the middle of what those neighbours actually earn, not at the top.
No. Platform commission, cleaning and our 15% + VAT management fee are all taken from bookings. With no bookings there's nothing deducted. Your only fixed costs are the ones you already have — mortgage, council tax and bills.
Every guest pays a £250–£400 security deposit and passes an ID check against other hosts' records before they receive check-in details. A 2–3 night minimum stay filters out most party bookings. Anything bigger than the deposit goes through the platform's host damage cover, and we handle the claim — you don't deal with Airbnb yourself.
Our honest advice is not to promote it. It adds a fire risk without winning bookings — guests aren't paying for heating, so they'll turn the radiators up rather than buy and burn wood. It isn't a feature that lifts your rate in this market; parking, a garden workspace, an EV charger or being pet-friendly do more. If you want extra peace of mind, a simple lockable guard plus a clear house rule covers the remaining risk.
We go ahead with work up to £300 in any month (one job or several added together). Anything above that, we ask you first. Small, low-urgency jobs are batched so one tradesperson visit covers several — cheaper for you. Emergencies like a leak are dealt with straight away. The cleaner checks the house after every stay, with fuller inspections two or three times a year. Garden upkeep is arranged ad hoc through our gardener contacts.
Yes. The loft, shed and the small utility room are ideal to lock — the utility room can double as our linen store. Locking a whole bedroom is possible too, but the listing would then advertise fewer bedrooms, which lowers what the house can earn.
Yes. Send photos of those rooms over WhatsApp and Zac will tell you honestly whether they'd hold back the figures above, and what we'd recommend. Where work is needed, we can recommend or arrange trades on a quoted basis — you'd approve any cost first.
Six months fixed, then a rolling three-month notice period. The notice period exists so guests who have already booked can be looked after before the house comes back to you.
Year 1 is the real risk. A brand-new listing has no reviews, so we plan on 80% of the full projection: about £14,330 net, or £1,194 a month on average. That is below your £1,500/month target, and the quiet months (September at around £634 in Year 1) will be well below it. Bad months happen and they don't carry over — the money simply isn't there that month. If demand underperformed badly for a sustained period, you could give notice after the first six months and long-let instead (Leicester 3-beds average around £1,045/month rent). What we don't do is make up the shortfall: nobody can guarantee bookings.
