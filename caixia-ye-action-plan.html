1. Where you actually stand

Before anything else, an accurate picture of your current position — including a correction to something I told you on the call.

⚠️ An important correction from our call

On our call I told you that bookings of 28 nights or more are exempt from London's 90-night rule, and that moving your property onto Stayful's management account would reset your 90 nights. I also said direct bookings through our own site wouldn't count. I've checked all three properly since, and I got them wrong. I'd rather correct that now than have you make a decision on it.

The rule comes from section 25 of the Greater London Council (General Powers) Act 1973, as amended by the Deregulation Act 2015. What it actually says:

  • The exempt stay length is 90 consecutive nights, not 28. A 28-night booking still counts toward your annual 90.
  • The 90 nights attach to the property, not the listing or the account. A new listing resets Airbnb's automatic counter, but not your legal position. If two operators let the same property in one year, both count toward the same 90.
  • Direct bookings count too. The limit applies across every channel. A platform not being able to see a booking isn't the same as that booking being exempt.

This matters because the plan we sketched out on the call — forcing 28-night minimums — would not have protected you. The rest of this plan is built on the correct position.

💡 And some genuinely good news

You told me you'd spent ages hunting for a "short-let licence" on Brent's website and that Brent's own staff didn't know what you were talking about. That's because there is no short-let licence in England — you were both using different words for different things. What you need is a planning application for change of use, made to Brent's planning department, not licensing. That one word should unblock you straight away.

2. What genuinely fixes this

There are two real routes, and one natural reset. All three are worth using together.

Route 1 — Planning permission for change of use (the real fix)

This is the only thing that removes the 90-night ceiling permanently. You apply to Brent Council's planning department for permission to change the use of the flat from residential (C3) to short-term letting use. If granted, the cap no longer applies to your property.

What to expect honestly: it is a planning application, not a form-filling exercise. Neighbours are consulted by post — you raised a worry about that given you're in a tower block, and the honest answer is yes, they will be written to, and objections are possible. London boroughs under housing pressure refuse a meaningful proportion of these. Brent may also ask about noise, waste and access. It is worth doing, and it is worth being realistic that it may not be granted.

We'd strongly suggest a short conversation with a local planning consultant before you submit — they'll tell you quickly whether your building and block make it viable.

Route 2 — Genuine long stays of 90+ consecutive nights

A single continuous stay of 90 nights or more to the same occupier falls outside the definition of temporary sleeping accommodation, so it doesn't count toward your 90 at all. This is the legitimate version of what we discussed on the call — the threshold is just 90, not 28.

Being straight about the trade-off: this is a mid-let or corporate-let business, not a nightly one. Nightly rates on 90+ night stays are materially lower than short-stay rates, turnover is minimal, and the guest profile is relocations, contractors and corporate placements rather than tourists. It produces steadier, lower income. It is not the business you've been running since June, and you should decide on that basis.

Route 3 — The 1 January 2027 reset

The 90-night count runs by calendar year and resets on 1 January regardless of what else happens. From January you have a fresh 90 nights of short-let capacity. That gives you roughly three to four months of normal nightly operation in early 2027 — which is a sensible window in which to have the planning application resolved.

Two things to check before we go live

Your lease. You mentioned you're in a tower block. Leases on blocks like yours very commonly prohibit lettings shorter than six months, and short-letting in breach of a lease covenant has been upheld by the courts. This is separate from the council question and can put your lease at risk on its own. Please check your lease, or send it to us and we'll flag anything obvious.

The council tax condition. The statutory exemption has two limbs, not one: the 90-night limit and a requirement that at least one person providing the accommodation is liable for council tax at the property. We want to confirm how that reads where Stayful is operating the listing before we commit either of us to anything. We'll come back to you on this.
