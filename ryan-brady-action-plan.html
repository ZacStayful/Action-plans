What drives demand around St James Place West

🌊

Plymouth Hoe & the Barbican

St James Place West sits just behind the Hoe, a few minutes' walk from the seafront, the Barbican's harbour, restaurants and bars. It is the part of the city leisure visitors want to stay in, which is why 12 active Airbnbs sit within half a kilometre of you.

🎭

Theatre Royal & Plymouth Pavilions

The Theatre Royal and the Pavilions are both a short walk away. Touring shows and gigs bring couples and groups in for one and two-night stays, many from across Devon and Cornwall.

⚓

Devonport naval base & defence work

HMNB Devonport and the Babcock dockyard are among the region's biggest employers and bring a steady flow of contractors and visiting staff. Contractors want a full kitchen and space for 2–4 people, which a 2-bed flat gives them.

🏥

Hospital & University

University Hospitals Plymouth (Derriford) and the University of Plymouth generate visiting clinicians, locums, families of patients, open days and graduations through the year.

⛴️

Millbay ferry port

Brittany Ferries sails from Millbay, close to the flat, to Roscoff and Santander. Travellers often stay the night before an early sailing or after a late arrival.

🥾

Gateway to the coast and Dartmoor

The South West Coast Path runs along the Hoe, and Dartmoor is under an hour away. Walkers and families use central Plymouth as a base, alongside the National Marine Aquarium on the Barbican.

Your questions, answered honestly

Yes. As a co-freeholder (and a party to the lease) your brother needs to know before you switch, and the first job is to read the lease for any clause restricting holiday or short lets. On the call I said you didn't need to tell anyone, then corrected that for your brother. I also said that even if you didn't change the lease nobody would pull you up on it. I'd rather you didn't rely on that: if the lease restricts short lets, get it formally varied (a solicitor can do a deed of variation) so it is clean for any future remortgage or sale. With a 50/50 split, neither of you can simply overrule the other, so agreement is the practical route. The strongest argument is that his flat would see very similar numbers to yours.
There is no short-let licence in England at the moment, and nothing we know of in Plymouth that stops you letting a flat on a short-term basis. Two honest caveats: the government has consulted on a national registration scheme and planning changes for short lets, so this may change; and once a property is available for 140+ days a year and actually let for 70+ days, it moves from council tax to business rates. We'll keep you posted on both.
This is the most important one to get right. Standard residential and buy-to-let mortgages usually don't allow holiday letting, so you'll want a holiday-let mortgage (or your lender's written consent) at the renewal. Holiday-let lenders typically assess on projected holiday-let income, and the rates can be a little higher than buy-to-let. We can't recommend a product, but we can give your broker the income analysis to support the application. You'll also want buildings insurance that covers short lets.
The running costs are: our management fee, cleaning, utilities, council tax, insurance and repairs all come off before tax. The bit I want to correct is the mortgage. The furnished holiday let tax regime ended in April 2025, so short lets are now taxed like other rental income. As an individual you don't deduct mortgage interest in full; you get a 20% tax credit on it instead. In a limited company it is different. Please check your own position with your accountant.
