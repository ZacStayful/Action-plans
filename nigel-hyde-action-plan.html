Straight answers

Everything you asked, answered honestly

What is the honest worst case if the projections are not hit?+

The honest worst case is that the ramp takes twelve months instead of six to nine, and Year 1 lands nearer £30,000 net than £39,099. That would happen if the photography under-sells the property, the early pricing is wrong, or a run of weak early reviews holds the ranking back.

Even that scenario is comfortably ahead of the £21,017 long-let benchmark, and well ahead of an empty annexe. But I am not going to pretend the projection is a floor — it is a projection built from twelve comparable properties, and there is no guarantee attached to it. The agreement says the same thing in plainer legal language, and it is worth reading that clause.

What I would say is that the risk here sits lower than most properties I look at, for a specific reason: the competing listings average only 67 reviews. You are not trying to out-rank an established operator.

Will I have to register for VAT on this?+

My view, which I gave you on the call: probably not. The turnover of £93,984 sits with Stayful, because we are the party providing the accommodation service to guests. What reaches you is a payout of around £48,874 for providing the property, and that figure sits below the VAT registration threshold.

But I need to be straight with you — that is my commercial understanding, not tax advice, and I am not qualified to give tax advice. The way this is structured genuinely matters to the answer. Please put it to your accountant before you rely on it. If it turns out I am wrong on your particular circumstances, better to know now than at year end.

Do I need to buy linen?+

No. We arrange linen on a hire model through the cleaner, and the cost is folded into the changeover fee that guests pay as part of the cleaning charge.

The reason I pushed you away from buying it: you would need three complete sets minimum, and short-let linen gets stained and retired far faster than you would expect. Buying means recurring replacement costs landing on you unpredictably. Hiring means a known figure that washes through the pricing. Keep your money for the sofa bed.

Should I put in a hot tub?+

Not yet. It would absolutely improve the property — I am not going to claim otherwise. But it adds a category of operational risk that is disproportionate to the return.

The specific problem is review exposure. A property can be immaculate in every respect, but if the hot tub is not perfect on arrival you will get a bad review for it, and that is not fair but it is what happens. Add the chemical management on top and it becomes real ongoing work for you.

My suggestion: go live without it. If we are six months in and not tracking toward these figures, we revisit it as a lever we still have in reserve. That is a better position than spending the money up front and finding you did not need to.

What about the 4.3m bifold doors — do I need to cover them?+

Less essential for you than it would be for most properties, for two reasons. The courtyard is genuinely private, so guests are not overlooked. And because it is a four-bedroom house we are not putting a sofa bed in the living room, so nobody is sleeping in there needing darkness in the morning.

That said, I am not going to tell you it would not be a positive — it would. My honest recommendation is to go live without it and see whether it comes up in guest feedback. If it does, get custom blinds made then. If it does not, you have saved the cost of a bespoke 4.3m solution.

Is the sofa bed really worth it if I have brand new beds?+

Yes, and it is the one fit-out item I would push on. You currently sleep nine. A sofa bed takes you to ten, and ten is the threshold that changes which searches you appear in.

For a four-bedroom, two-bathroom house, ten to twelve is the band you want to be in. Groups searching for a property for ten will not see a listing that sleeps nine at all — it is a filter, not a preference. That single item widens your addressable market meaningfully.

Your existing configuration is otherwise fine. Zip-and-link doubles would have been marginally better than fixed doubles for flexibility, but not enough to justify replacing new beds.

Do I have to use your cleaner, or can I use the one I found?+

Use yours if they pass. Pass their details over and we will assess them against our framework — standards, reliability, insurance, availability for changeovers on our schedule. If they meet it, they go on the list and are considered first for Park House.

The ~£90 a turnover you were quoted at £22.50 an hour over four hours is a fair local rate for labour. Bear in mind that excludes linen, which is why we wrap the whole changeover into a single cost we can charge on to the guest.

Being frank about why I want more than one option: cleaners do occasionally need replacing when standards slip, and having vetted alternatives already on file for your area makes that a phone call rather than a crisis.

Who actually comes out to the property?+

The photographer will visit, and they are based far closer to you than I am — I am in Leicestershire. The cleaner will visit to quote and assess the changeover. After go-live we inspect somewhere between twice and four times a year.

Since you live next door, you will realistically have better day-to-day visibility of the building than any inspection regime gives. That is an advantage, not a problem — and it is also why I said we can lean on your own trades for maintenance if you would rather.

What is the minimum stay, and can turnovers work around it?+

We start at a two-night minimum and adjust from there based on what the booking data shows. Some properties end up at five nights minimum because that is where the numbers land. We never run one-night stays — the cleaning cost per revenue pound is poor and the risk profile is worse.

Changeovers get scheduled around confirmed bookings, so the cleaner works to the calendar rather than a fixed day.

Can I block dates for friends and family?+

Yes, freely and at no charge. You go into the live calendar, select the dates and block them — provided nobody has already booked those nights, which is the only constraint.

One thing to remember: post a note in your Slack channel when you block dates so we know a clean is needed afterwards. The changeover cost falls to you for a personal stay since there is no guest to charge it to, but there is no lost management fee or penalty.

Why is the management fee 15% when I keep less than half the gross?+

This was the question you opened with, and it is the right one. The honest answer is that the 15% + VAT is only one of three deductions, and it is the only one that changes if you self-manage.

The platform commission of £14,100 goes to Airbnb and Booking.com whether we are involved or not. Cleaning and laundry of roughly £19,022 is an owner cost either way — and importantly, the cleaning fee guests pay is included in the gross figure, so part of that deduction is money that was never really yours to begin with.

The management fee itself is £11,988 a year, about £999 a month. That is the actual price of the difference between taking a payout and running the operation yourself. You concluded on the call that it was worth it at this income level, and I think you were right — but the number is there for you to test.
