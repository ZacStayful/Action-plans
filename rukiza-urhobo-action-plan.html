What drives demand around Granville Street

🎤
Events on your doorstep
The ICC, Symphony Hall and Utilita Arena are a short walk away around Broad Street and Brindleyplace, the O2 Academy is close by, and the NEC is about ten minutes by train from New Street. Concerts, conferences and shows bring a steady flow of one- and two-night stays, and the Frankfurt Christmas Market fills the city every November and December — part of why your November forecast is so strong.
🏢
Business district & contractors
Brindleyplace, the Mailbox and the city's office core are within walking distance. Corporate visitors and contractors on long-running regeneration and infrastructure projects (as we discussed on the call) want a self-contained flat with a kitchen rather than a hotel room — and these are the guests most likely to rebook direct.
🏥
Hospitals
Queen Elizabeth Hospital Birmingham is around 3km away in Edgbaston, and Birmingham Children's Hospital is in the city centre. Visiting families, agency staff and medical professionals book week-long stays all year round, which steadies the quieter months.
🎓
Universities
The University of Birmingham, Birmingham City University and Aston University all draw visitors for open days, graduations and academic events. Five Ways station, close to you, is one stop from University station on the Cross-City line.
🚆
Easy to reach
New Street — one of the busiest stations in the UK — is a walk away, with direct trains across the country, and Birmingham Airport is roughly 15 minutes by train. Guests can arrive without a car, which matters for a city-centre flat.
⭐
Room to stand out
The 12 nearby listings average a 4.6 rating with around 50 reviews each — that's beatable. As we said on the call, a well-run listing that looks after guests and chases reviews can start ranking well quite quickly in this patch.

Your questions, answered honestly

Fair challenge. £151 is the average across 12 live Airbnb listings within 0.18km, and the market average is actually £158 — so we're already pitched below it. The one we looked at on the call, likely in your building, is charging £200+ a night and is almost fully booked, but it's a Guest Favourite with years of reviews, which you won't have on day one. To start, we'll price a little under the market to win the first bookings and reviews, then let the rate climb. As a rule of thumb, every £10 off the nightly rate takes roughly £1,400 a year off your net. That's why Year 1 in this plan is already set at 80% of the forecast — £3,138 below full run rate, which is more cushion than a £10 rate cut.
You were right to be sceptical of 87%. We've projected 54% — below the 57% average of the 12 listings next to you. One of those listings does hit 87%, but that's the best performer in the area, not something to plan around. We'd rather show you a number you can rely on and beat it.
There's no guarantee. With your mortgage (~£485) and bills (~£400) at around £885 a month, here's how it looks: at full run rate you'd keep about £423 a month after those costs; in Year 1 at 80%, about £161 a month on average — tighter, and the quietest Year 1 months (May and February) dip slightly below your costs. If Year 1 came in at 60% of the forecast (£9,415 net), you'd be roughly £1,200 short over the year — about £100 a month. A small correction from the call: I said February would be the quietest month at around £900 — the analysis actually puts May lowest at £992, with February at £1,015 at full run rate. If it genuinely isn't working after the 6-month initial term, you can give 3 months' notice and switch to a long let. Moving to business rates once the letting thresholds are met can also take council tax off your bills.
The guest pays the nightly rate plus a cleaning fee in one payment, and all of it shows in the gross. Say £100 a night for two nights plus a £150 clean: the guest pays £350, the £150 goes straight out to the cleaner, and the nightly income is yours (less platform and management fees). So cleaning never comes out of your pocket — and the £15,691 net figure is already after it. That net is what you'd expect to receive as payouts.
An initial 6-month term, so we have time to deliver the figures, then it rolls on 3 months' notice either side — the notice is so guests already booked can still stay and the handover is smooth. To be upfront: the agreement includes a £1,000 early exit fee if you leave within the first 6 months. There's no onboarding fee. Our fee is 15% + VAT, already included in the net figures, plus a £42 monthly software fee under the agreement.
Airbnb, Booking.com and our own direct booking site. We deliberately don't spread across seven platforms: guests can only review where they booked, and every extra platform blocks dates on the big ones. Concentrating on Airbnb builds your reviews and ranking faster. Over time, contractors, hospital and university visitors are the guests we convert to direct bookings, which cuts platform fees.
Your broker's lenders are happy with standard rental figures for now. If that changes and they want short-let numbers — a high, medium and low projection — just email me and I'll sort it.
