Your income picture

Before anything else — a correction on the London 90-night rule.

On the call Zac said two things that weren't right. He said you'd need a licence, and that stays of 28 nights or more don't count towards the 90 nights.

The accurate position: there is no short-let licence in London. The rule is planning law. Without planning permission for change of use from Havering Council, a home in Greater London can be let as short-term accommodation for no more than 90 nights in a calendar year — and "short-term" means any stay of fewer than 90 consecutive nights. So a 28-night contractor stay still counts. Only stays of 90 nights or longer fall outside the cap.

Why this matters: the £37,521 below assumes the house can be let short-term all year. That holds if you have planning permission. If you don't, the realistic model is 90 nights of short stays plus three-month-plus stays for the rest of the year — which, as it happens, is exactly the relocation and insurance market you told us you want. Zac will rework the figures for that scenario with you on the next call. You asked how to "get around" the restriction; the honest answer is you can't, but you can build around it.
