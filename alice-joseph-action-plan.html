What drives demand around Lind Close

Based only on what we can verify about Reading and the surrounding area.

🚆
Rail and Elizabeth line links

Reading is the western end of the Elizabeth line and has fast services to London Paddington. Earley station is also close by. That suits business travellers and visitors who want a central base for 2 to 5 night stays.

🎓
University of Reading

The Whiteknights campus sits partly in Earley and is under 2 km from the house. Parents visiting students, graduation weeks, open days and academic guests all need somewhere to stay.

🎪
Reading Festival

One of England's biggest music festivals is held in Reading every summer, which is when the model has your best months.

💼
Commercial and contractor market

Reading is a major commercial centre, particularly for IT and insurance. One of the nearby comparable listings is marketed directly to contractors, so there is demand for longer work stays.

👨‍👩‍👧‍👦
Groups and families

Nearby comparables sleeping large groups (one of them sleeps 9) earn £290 to £422 a night at 52 to 54% occupancy. A 5 to 6 bedroom house suits families and groups who want to stay together.

🚗
Road access

The M4 is about 1.5 miles from the university's Whiteknights campus, which makes the area easy to reach by car for visitors from London, Oxford and the south coast.

The exact mix of guests varies. We tailor the listing to the people most likely to book: transport links, the university and the space for groups all go in the listing.

Your questions, answered honestly

No. Guest messaging, check-ins, cleaning coordination, pricing, maintenance coordination and reviews are all handled by us. What you are responsible for is the mortgage, the bills (water, gas, electricity, internet), two key safes (about £15 each) and approving any maintenance spend that goes above £300 in a month. You'll see everything in your booking calendar and a shared Slack or WhatsApp channel, get a statement on the 1st of every month with the payout by the 5th, and a quarterly video review call.

This is the real trade-off with a short let, and it's worth planning for. The income is not a fixed rent: it moves month to month with the seasons and with how many reviews the listing has. On our modelled numbers the quietest month at full run rate is about £3,277 and in Year 1 (at 80%) about £2,622, but a model is not a guarantee, and on the call Zac was clear that an individual month could come in a lot lower. The sensible way to run it is to hold a cash cushion in the first year so one slow month, or a boiler, doesn't put pressure on your mortgage. Maintenance is paid from your income, and we flag anything over £300 in a month with you before we go ahead.

The listing is brand new, so for roughly the first six months expect to run around 20% below the forecast while the first reviews come in. That is already built into the Year 1 figure of £39,390. The worst case beyond that is that reviews build more slowly than we expect, or demand in your area is softer than the comparables suggest, and the property earns closer to (or for a few months below) the £2,700 a month you get now. Nothing in the numbers is guaranteed. We do not offer guaranteed rent, so you carry the income risk. You have a 6-month minimum term (see below) and you can leave on 3 months' notice.

On the call Zac confirmed there is no licensing requirement stopping you. Letting the whole house to a single booking at a time is not the same as a room-by-room HMO. We'd still suggest you check three things yourself before going live: your mortgage lender's consent, your buildings and contents insurance, and any restrictions in your title. Fire safety is not a licensing requirement for short lets in England, but it is good practice, and you already have smoke alarms, fire blankets and extinguishers in place.

Reviews are the biggest driver of how fast a new listing climbs. We make sure the stay is good, follow up with guests who haven't left a review, and offer small discounts to guests who rebook direct. Our experience is roughly one in two or three stays turns into a review. Based on the 12 nearby comparables, the area average is around 60 reviews at 5.0 stars. You should reach ~10 reviews (about 85% of forecast) at around month 6, and it takes roughly 8 to 11 months to reach the area average.

Bookings that come through Airbnb or Booking.com are handled by us and you wouldn't normally see who is staying. We have taken council-placed and similar bookings before and we have deposits, ID checks and house rules in place to limit the risk. If you'd rather not take that kind of booking at all, tell Zac before you sign so it can be agreed in writing rather than assumed.

Every guest pays a security deposit (£200 to £400) and their ID is checked before check-in details are released, and house rules cover noise and parking. We'd also recommend a Minut noise and occupancy sensor. It measures decibel levels and how many devices are on the WiFi, has no camera, and is legal for that reason. If a neighbour ever phones you, message us and we deal with the guest straight away. We can't promise nothing will ever happen, but this is the set-up that works.

There is a 6-month fixed term, then it rolls month to month. Notice is 3 months so that guests who've already booked are honoured. There is a £1,000 early-exit penalty only if you leave inside the first 6 months. There's no onboarding or setup fee from us, and no exit fee after the term.
