Your questions, answered honestly

Do I need permission from my freeholder or shared-equity partner before short-letting?
Honestly — probably. As a leasehold property, Guinness Housing Association will usually need to consent to short-let use, and in a fair number of cases that consent doesn't get granted. Your shared-equity partner may have views too. We've flagged this clearly — it's a genuine risk, and proceeding anyway is your decision, not something we can clear on your behalf.
Is the £508/month figure tied to my actual mortgage payment?
No. It's a general revenue projection based on nightly rates and occupancy for a property like yours in this location — completely independent of whatever you personally pay each month on the mortgage.
Would I make more money long-letting instead?
On the numbers alone, yes — long-let would net around £819/month against roughly £508/month short-let. But it isn't really an option for you: your mortgage type doesn't permit long-term letting, and it would remove the flexibility to use the flat yourself, which matters to you.
What's the honest worst case if these projections aren't hit?
The £508/month average is a projection based on comparable listings nearby, not a guarantee. If occupancy or rates land lower than forecast — more likely before you've built up reviews — some months could sit closer to the £370–400 range we're seeing for January/February. Even at that low end, it's still income against a property you're paying for regardless of whether it's let.
How does personal-use booking actually work day to day?
You get full calendar access and can block any dates, with no cap on how many days a year you use it. The main practical point: the more notice you give, the easier it is to fit around confirmed guest bookings — last-minute blocks are possible but more likely to clash with something already booked in.
If something breaks and a contractor is needed, who pays?
You do, as the property owner — that's a normal cost of ownership. As a rule of thumb, maintenance tends to run around 5% of revenue for a flat (typically less than for a house). Non-urgent jobs get batched together to keep costs down; urgent ones cost more because guest experience and reviews come first.
