Where the £213 comes from

The top four comparable listings around you take most of the revenue — roughly £68k, £46k, £46k and £43k a year — and they average around 68 reviews. Most other listings sit in the £20k–£30k range. With around 10 reviews, the Coach House is competing against far more established listings, which is why you've had to drop to £120–£130 to win bookings. That's normal for a listing in its first year, not a sign the property can't perform.

What changes things: getting past 20–40 good reviews, and keeping Airbnb and Booking.com balanced so one doesn't crowd the other out. Seasonality still applies — winter weekdays will stay quieter, summer and school holidays are where the rate headroom is.

Leicester — the figures you asked for

As discussed on the call for the 2-bed penthouse at Yeoman Street: roughly £40,000 gross and £20,000–£22,000 net a year (a little under £2,000 a month), at around 60% occupancy and £180 a night.

Against a £1,500/month mortgage (£18,000 a year), that leaves very little once council tax and bills are paid. You were right to park it — the Coach House is the stronger case, so this plan focuses there.

Your questions, answered honestly

How do you get to £213 a night when I'm currently at £120–£130?+
Honestly — not straight away. The £213 comes from 12 active Airbnb comparables around Chinley. The listings earning most (£43k–£68k a year) have around 68 reviews on average; you have about 10. With few reviews, dropping the price is how a new listing wins bookings, and that's exactly what you've been doing. Rates climb as reviews build — that's why Year 1 is shown at 80% of the full figure. Our pricing is dynamic, so we push rates up as the listing earns the right to them, rather than discounting to £80 a night to fill the calendar like your previous operator did.
Can I see the data myself?+
Yes. The four Stayful properties in section 3 are real Airbnb platform figures for Jan–Dec 2025, with full case studies you can open and print. 803 Eastbank Tower in Manchester is the example I couldn't load on the call — it's the one we use to show how projections compare with what actually happened. On the next call I can share the comparable listings behind your figures screen by screen.
Will bookings be direct or still Airbnb?+
A mix. For the Coach House, I'd expect around 10–20% direct once the listing is established — not the 20–40% I mentioned early in the call. Your guest base is mainly one type (walkers and leisure), so the focus first is ranking well on Airbnb and Booking.com, then converting repeat and work guests to direct where we can.
Why is Booking.com getting most of my bookings?+
Because both platforms share one calendar. Whichever platform books first gets rewarded with more visibility, and every night it takes is a night Airbnb can't sell — so Airbnb gradually shows you less. We manage both channels plus Google and direct together, so no single platform crowds out the others.
Can I keep my own cleaners?+
Yes — we'd rather you did. Your cleaners already know the property. We message them directly (WhatsApp), they're notified when a clean is due and send photos afterwards. On your single-cleaner worry at the Coach House: we have cleaning contacts across the country we can line up as backup cover, so one person being ill doesn't leave a guest without a clean property.
What does it actually cost me — the bit you don't see?+
Our fee is 15% + VAT. The net figure of £25,642 a year is after everything: platform commissions (£7,396), cleaning and laundry (£8,875) and management (£7,396). There's a £42/month software fee too. What's left for you to cover is your mortgage (£560/month), council tax, utilities and WiFi.
What happens to my existing listing and reviews?+
Stayful runs listings on its own host accounts, which carry their own review history. How your current listing and its reviews are handled during the move is something I'll go through with you properly on our next call before you commit to anything.
What is the honest worst case if projections are not hit?+
If the listing never gets past where it is today — weekends booked, slow weekdays, £120–£130 a night — your income stays roughly where it is now, minus our fee. That's why the agreement has a 6-month initial term, then a rolling 3-month notice period: if it isn't working, you're not locked in for years. Even at Year 1 levels (£20,514 net) the property covers its £560 mortgage more than three times over.

Why spring matters

No pressure on timing — you said yourself we're heading into the quiet months. The point is the opposite: winter is when reviews get built cheaply, so the listing is ranked properly before the walking season and summer, where the rate headroom is.
