What drives demand around Alexandra Terrace

🏰
Cathedral, Castle & the Cathedral Quarter
Lincoln Cathedral and Lincoln Castle — home to one of the four surviving 1215 Magna Carta copies — are the city's anchor attractions, and Alexandra Terrace is within walking distance of both. As you said on the call, this is who books here: couples and families visiting the castle and cathedral, and exploring Steep Hill and Bailgate.
🎓
University of Lincoln
Graduations, open days, move-in and move-out weekends bring parents and visiting families who want a whole home rather than a hotel room — a good fit for a 2-bed that sleeps up to 6.
🏥
Lincoln County Hospital
The county's main hospital brings visiting families, agency and contract staff who need a few nights to a few weeks nearby — steady mid-week demand that doesn't depend on the tourist calendar.
🚆
Direct trains to London
LNER runs direct Lincoln–London King's Cross services (roughly two hours), which makes Lincoln an easy weekend break from London and the South East, as well as supporting business travel.
🏭
Engineering & business travel
Lincoln has a sizeable engineering base, including Siemens Energy's turbine operation, which brings visiting engineers and contractors — often longer mid-week stays that help fill the quieter months.
🎄
Christmas in Lincoln — corrected
On the call I pointed to the Lincoln Christmas Market. To be accurate: the big four-day street market was last held in 2022 and has been scrapped by the council. Lincoln Cathedral now runs a smaller, ticketed market (3–5 December 2026) alongside city-wide festive events. There's still a December lift, but it's smaller than the old market's reputation suggests — so don't bank on Christmas to carry a December launch.
Direct booking score for this location: 82/100 — repeat and off-platform guests are the long-term fix for quiet winters, but they take time to build.

Your questions, answered straight

A correction first: our management fee is 15% + VAT, not a flat 15% — I should have said that on the call. It's calculated on what Airbnb (or Booking.com) pays out after its commission, not on the gross booking value. You asked specifically about cleaning fees after your Houst experience, so to be completely straight: the payout includes the cleaning fee guests pay, and our fee is calculated before the cleaning cost comes off — so yes, the 15% + VAT does apply to that portion. It doesn't stack on top of the gross the way some managers charge. The agreement also has a £42/month software fee. The income figures in this plan are after platform commission, cleaning & laundry and management.
You said yourself it's almost certain that at some point you'll think "we could have done this ourselves" — so here are the exact terms. The agreement starts with a six-month initial term; leaving inside that term carries a £1,000 early-exit fee. After that it rolls on three months' written notice from either side. We'd hand over bookings in the calendar and the cleaning contacts would stay in Lincoln. Read the agreement (linked below) before deciding — it's the document that governs, not this summary.
Every booking goes through screening: ID and a background/review-history check where available (what other hosts have said about the guest), a £100 security deposit, and a no-party policy we enforce against the deposit. We've managed in Lincoln since 2022 without a party issue, and your stretch of the city draws castle-and-cathedral visitors rather than the nightlife crowd. If you want belt and braces — the approach you use in Bristol and Bath — a noise sensor on the property alerts you if levels stay above a threshold, and we deal with it. My honest view is to start without and add one if needed.
You're right to push on this. £1,266 is the modelled January for an established listing. A brand-new listing going live in December or February has no reviews yet, so its first January could be well below that — near zero is possible. That's exactly why the Year 1 figures here are 80% of the model, and why the ramp below puts months 1–3 at the lowest level. Plan the mortgage so Dec–Feb in year one doesn't have to carry it. Direct bookings (82/100 score for this location) are the longer-term lever for smoothing winter.
Guest-caused damage comes out of the deposit (or a platform claim) — you won't be chased for it. Genuine wear and tear gets split in two: urgent items are handled straight away; minor ones are batched so you're not paying separate call-out charges. Once maintenance reaches £300 in a month, you approve anything further — so no surprise £1,000 bill. If you already trust particular contractors, we'll coordinate them instead of ours.
You block dates yourself in the channel manager calendar, drop a note in the Slack channel ("we're in on these dates, can you sort a clean after checkout?"), and we book it. The clean appears as a line item on your monthly statement.
The realistic downside isn't one bad month — it's a slow first year. If reviews build slowly, Year 1 could land below the £21,950 shown here, with the winter months doing the least. What protects you: 12 established comparables within 0.4 km show demand is proven at this location; you can review every booking in real time; and after the initial six months you can leave on three months' notice. What doesn't: there's no income guarantee — these are projections, not promises. And every figure here is before your mortgage payments.
