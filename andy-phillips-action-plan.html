Frequently asked questions

Does my current buildings & contents insurance cover short-let, or do I need a new policy before we switch?
Most owners we work with keep their existing buildings & contents cover — Stayful's own protection already covers a lot of the guest-damage scenarios, so your policy is mainly there for the general wear-and-tear cases it always covered. It's worth a quick call to your insurer when your renewal comes round in September to flag the short-let use and ask if anything changes, but this is rarely a blocker, and if a short-let-specific quote comes back expensive, most owners simply keep what they've got.
How easily will a property like mine actually let?
Based on what you've described — two off-street parking spaces, a large garden, the new summer house with its cinema setup, a downstairs toilet plus upstairs bathroom, and the flexible workspace/sofa-bed option — you're ticking most of the boxes guests search for in this area, and several of those features are genuinely rare among comparable listings nearby. That said, first-year momentum always depends on reviews building up, which is why we build in the ~20% Year 1 buffer rather than promising full run-rate from day one.
Should the shed be used for management supplies or converted into guest storage?
Since you've already got a large understairs cupboard for cleaning products and kit, we'd suggest turning the shed into guest storage instead — somewhere for garden furniture and toys over winter, and a genuine selling point for families who don't want to pack the car full of kids' games. That's the plan we've noted for your listing.
Is the all-in service cost a fixed monthly figure or does it move with revenue?
It's a percentage of revenue, not a flat monthly charge — so it naturally scales down in quieter months and up in busy ones. The only separate item is maintenance: general wear-and-tear repairs are only flagged to you if a single bill (or the cumulative total in a month) goes over £300, at which point we'll always check with you first before proceeding further.
Should we keep the upstairs carpet or switch to wood flooring before listing?
Keep the carpet. A professional clean will make a bigger visible difference than the cost and disruption of replacing it with wood flooring, and carpet tends to hide the kind of minor guest wear that would show up more obviously on hard flooring. This is one of the lower-priority items on your pre-listing list.
What's the honest worst case if these projections aren't hit?
Being direct about this: short-let income does fluctuate, and the figures above are estimates, not guarantees. The worst month we'd typically expect for a property like yours is around £1,100 net in a quiet month like February — even in Year 1, with the ramp-up buffer applied, that's still a livable floor rather than a cliff edge. The bigger risk in your specific situation isn't a single bad month, it's the review-building period in the first few months after you go live, which is exactly why we've built the ~20% Year 1 discount into every figure on this page rather than showing you the optimistic Year 2+ number and hoping it holds from day one.
