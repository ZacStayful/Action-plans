⚠ Before the numbers: a correction and one big condition

On the call I said London needs a short-let licence. That was wrong — you were right that there's nothing like that in place in Greenwich. There's no short-let licence in England.

What I didn't cover is the Greater London 90-night rule. A whole home in London can only be let as short stays for up to 90 nights per calendar year unless the council grants planning permission for a change of use. The rule applies to the property itself, across every platform and direct bookings, and fines can reach £20,000.

The projections below assume around 281 booked nights a year, so they only apply with planning permission. Capped at 90 nights, this flat would earn roughly £11,261 a year net — well below your £20,400 rent. I wouldn't recommend that route, and I'd rather you heard it from me now than later.

Three things that have to be true first

1. Planning permission. Needed for more than 90 nights a year. A planning consultant can tell you how Greenwich treats applications like this before you spend anything. Approval isn't guaranteed.

2. Your tenant. Since 1 May 2026 tenancies are periodic and Section 21 has gone. There's no possession ground for switching to short-let, so this only happens if your tenant chooses to move on — which fits with how you said you want to handle it.

3. Your lease. Many flat leases restrict lettings under a certain length. Worth checking yours, or asking the freeholder, early on.
