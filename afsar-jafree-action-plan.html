What drives demand in Canterbury

Six things generate bookings around Coppergate. These are drawn from the demand data for CT2, not generic marketing.

🏥
Kent & Canterbury Hospital

2.27km away. You know this world better than I do — visiting clinicians, locum cover and families staying near a patient. These are midweek 3–7 night bookings that don't care what season it is, and they are the reason Canterbury holds up in January and February.

⛪
Canterbury Cathedral & the old city

A UNESCO World Heritage Site drawing visitors year-round, and 2 Coppergate sits inside the walkable core. This is what fills weekends and drives the £323 average nightly rate — leisure guests booking a whole house rather than four hotel rooms.

🚆
Canterbury West station

0.17km from the door — about a two-minute walk. High Speed 1 puts you under an hour from St Pancras. For a group coming from London without a car, that walk-from-the-train convenience is the single thing that makes a listing get chosen over one a mile out.

🎓
University of Kent & Canterbury Christ Church

1.55km away. Not students as tenants any more — parents at open days, visiting academics, and the graduation weekends where every room in the city sells out at a premium. You get the demand the universities create without the tenancy risk that came with it.

🅿️
Two allocated spaces and a garage

Free on-site parking is genuinely scarce in central Canterbury and it is one of the strongest filters guests apply. On a 4-bed booked by families and groups arriving by car, this is a real advantage over most of the comparables — several of which have no parking at all.

🎟️
Events and venue demand

56 venues and event spaces within 5km, plus the Canterbury Festival and cricket at the St Lawrence Ground. These create the peak-rate weekends where a 4-bed with parking commands well above the average nightly rate.

Your questions, answered honestly

This is the right question and I'd rather answer it properly than reassure you. The £66,504 gross figure isn't a model or an algorithm's guess — it comes from 8 active listings within half a kilometre of Coppergate, pulled from real booking data. Two of them are doing more than we've projected for you: £67,464 and £70,054 gross. Several are doing less, and honestly I think that's down to how they're run rather than the properties themselves.

What makes estimates diverge so wildly between providers is usually one of two things: some quote gross and let you assume it's net, and some model 70–80% occupancy that simply isn't achievable. We've used 56% occupancy, which is below the 68% area average, and we've deducted everything — platform fees, cleaning, laundry and our management fee. The number you should hold onto is £34,581 net, and that's after all of it.

The honest caveat: no forecast is a promise. What I'd suggest is judging us on whether the assumptions are conservative rather than on whether the headline is impressive.

The worst case is roughly £2,000 a month net — that's what the model produces if every single month of the year performed like February, the quietest month. Realistically that never happens, because the seasonal spread is what makes short-letting work.

But here's the part that actually matters for your decision: £2,000 a month is essentially what the property already earns you as a student HMO at £2,100–£2,150. So the genuine downside isn't losing money — it's doing roughly what you're doing now, without the tenant disputes, the mid-contract notices, and the condition the house comes back in.

I won't give you a guarantee, because I can't honestly stand behind one. What I can tell you is that the floor of this model sits at your current ceiling.

Three layers, and I'll be straight about what each one is and isn't worth.

First, platform-level checks — ID verification and host review history through our channel manager. If a guest has been marked down by previous hosts, we see it before they book. This filters the obvious problems, not every problem.

Second, and this is the real deterrent: a security deposit of around £400 is held for every stay. It's refundable, but only after we've been in and inspected. If something's been damaged and the guest caused it, it comes out of that deposit before anything is returned.

Third, platform insurance. We only list on Airbnb, Booking.com and our own direct site precisely because all three carry cover for guest-caused damage. In six years I've had to claim once — a kitchen fire — and it took two to three months to pay out but it did pay out, and we handled the guest and the repair.

What this doesn't cover is fair wear and tear, which we deal with and deduct from your payout. Given you're spending around £40,000 on this refurbishment, that distinction matters: a smashed TV comes from the deposit, a worn carpet after two years doesn't.
