What drives demand in Worthing

Only things that are genuinely true of your location and your property — no filler.

🏖️
Worthing seafront & pier
Worthing is a Blue Flag stretch of the West Sussex coast with a Victorian pier, promenade and beach. Coastal leisure demand drives the June to August peak, and that is exactly where your strongest months sit.
🏥
Worthing Hospital
The hospital on Lyndhurst Road brings a steady flow of locum doctors, agency nurses and contractors needing weeks or months of accommodation rather than nights. This is the most reliable non-leisure demand in the area — and the closest match to the contract work you asked about.
⛰️
South Downs on your doorstep
High Salvington sits on the downs above the town, with Cissbury Ring and Highdown Hill within walking distance and the South Downs National Park immediately north. Walkers and cyclists book year-round, which softens the winter months.
🎡
Brighton overflow
Brighton is about eleven miles east. When Brighton sells out or prices spike for events, festivals and conferences, guests look along the coast — and Worthing is the first serious option they find.
🚂
London and Gatwick access
Direct trains run from Worthing to London Victoria in roughly an hour and a half, and Gatwick is around a thirty-five minute drive. That supports both weekend visitors from London and pre-flight stays.
🏠
Six bedrooms is genuinely scarce
Whole-house six-bed lets are rare almost everywhere, and Worthing is no exception. Large family groups, milestone celebrations, wedding parties and contractor teams all compete for the handful of properties that can sleep everyone under one roof — and they pay a premium for it.

Your questions, answered straight

You wanted guaranteed rent — why won't Stayful offer it?+

Because we won't promise something we can't control, and honestly, the operators who do offer it are pricing the risk into your rent. A guarantee means someone takes a margin for carrying the downside, and that margin comes out of your income every single month. What we do instead is show you the real projection, show you the low months as well as the peaks, and let you decide. The trade-off is straightforward: with a guarantee you get certainty and a lower ceiling; with us you get the full upside and you carry the seasonal variation. On your numbers the upside is substantial — but it is not guaranteed, and I'd rather you hear that from me now than be disappointed in February.

Can you actually get me contract stays for insurance workers?+

Partly, and I want to be precise about this. Insurance-placement and relocation stays are a real segment — families displaced by fire or flood, and contractors on multi-week placements. Worthing Hospital in particular generates steady medical-contractor demand. We do take direct and externally sourced bookings of this kind, and a six-bed house is well suited to them. What I cannot do is build your projection on them. The figures in this plan are based on platform short-let demand, which is measurable. Contract work sits on top of that as genuine upside when it comes, not as the foundation. Note that where a booking is sourced through a fee-bearing channel, a 5% or 10% plus VAT booking channel commission can apply on top of the management fee — that is set out in the agreement.

How does this run while I'm abroad?+

That is the part this model is actually built for. We handle guest communication, pricing, cleaning, linen, key and access management, maintenance coordination and platform relations. You get a Slack channel for day-to-day contact, live calendar access, and a statement between the 1st and 5th of each month with your payout in the first three working days. You do not deal with guests and you do not deal with Airbnb. What stays with you: utilities and broadband in your name, council tax or business rates, insurance, and authorising anything above £300. Time zones are not a problem — nothing needs your input in real time.

What is the honest worst case if the projections aren't hit?+

Here it is plainly. Your cost base is about £3,500 a month, roughly £42,000 a year. Year one net is projected at £33,158. If we hit that exactly, you are still around £8,800 short across the year. If we come in ten percent under projection, that gap widens to about £12,100. That is the honest downside and it is the reason I gave you the advice I did on the call rather than just selling you the headline. If you get your costs down to roughly £2,200 a month, year one turns into a surplus of about £6,750 and a ten percent miss still leaves you comfortably positive. The projections are the variable I can't guarantee. Your cost base is the variable you can control — so control that one first.

What exactly did you mean about the mortgage and business rates?+

Two separate levers, both yours to pull, and together I estimated around £1,300 a month. First, the mortgage — if you are on a product that isn't right for a let property, or on a rate that can be improved, a broker should review it. Second, business rates — a property let commercially as short-stay accommodation may fall out of council tax and into business rates, and with small business rate relief the bill can drop substantially. Both need proper advice: a mortgage broker for the first, and your local authority or a rating adviser for the second. I am not a mortgage or tax adviser and the £1,300 is my estimate from the numbers you gave me, not a calculation you should rely on. But it is the single highest-value thing you can do on this deal, and it is worth two phone calls before you commit to anything.

Six bedrooms and large groups — what about damage?+

Larger properties do see more wear, and I won't pretend otherwise. Bookings run through Airbnb's AirCover, which carries damage protection up to £2.5m plus theft cover, and we manage claims on your behalf — you never argue with Airbnb yourself. We photograph before and after, vet guests, and enforce Stayful's house rules. Where damage is recoverable we pursue it through the platform or the guest. Where it isn't, honestly, that cost falls to you — that is in the agreement and I'd rather flag it than bury it. On the routine side, a six-bed needs mattresses reviewed every three years and soft furnishings refreshed on a similar cycle. Budget for it as a running cost, not a surprise.
