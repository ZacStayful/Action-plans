Your questions, answered honestly

Another agent gave me lower projections. Why should I believe these numbers?+
You should interrogate them — that is the right instinct. These figures come from 12 active Airbnb listings within a short radius of Mill House, not from a general area estimate. Those comparables average £34,653 gross a year at 60% occupancy and £186 a night. Your projection of £42,252 gross sits above that average because of the views and the outdoor space, but it is below the top four performers in your area (£47,225, £47,227, £48,545 and £50,007). The full comparable table is in Section 2 so you can check the working yourself. Where a lower quote usually comes from is either a different assumption about occupancy or a deduction of costs before showing you the headline — always ask which. And if the other agent's numbers are built on different comparables, ask them which properties.
What about change of use? The other agent said it could be a planning issue.+
I want to be straight with you: I did not have a confident answer for you on the call and I am not going to invent one now. What I know is that Cumbria has not, to my knowledge, imposed a formal change-of-use requirement for short lets in the way some other parts of the country have, and holiday letting is long established across the Lakes. But planning is a local authority matter and it does change. The right people to confirm it are Westmorland & Furness Council's planning team, and your conveyancing solicitor as part of the purchase — they should be checking permitted use anyway. I am also chasing a proper answer and will come back to you with it in writing. On the second point I raised: framing the property as a second home initially and looking at an SPV or limited company structure later is a conversation to have with an accountant, not with me — I am not qualified to advise on it and it has tax consequences worth getting right.
How does personal use actually work, and what does it cost me?+
You get owner access to the calendar and block the dates you want. Blocking flows out to every platform at once from the central dashboard, so you never have to touch Airbnb or Booking.com directly. The management fee does not change because of personal use — it is charged on what the property earns, so if you block a month you simply do not earn that month's income. The only real cost is the income you give up, and that varies enormously by month. Blocking February costs you roughly £1,394; blocking August costs you roughly £3,119. There is a month-by-month view of exactly that in Section 4. My honest advice: take the shoulder months and the odd midweek in summer, and you barely feel it.
I'm 4.5 hours away and I've never let a property. What do I actually have to do?+
Practically nothing day to day. We handle guest communication, pricing, cleaning and linen, check-in and check-out, maintenance coordination and the listings themselves. Your responsibilities are the ownership costs — utilities and WiFi in your name, council tax, insurance, and keeping the property in good repair. We do detailed inspections roughly twice a year plus checks after stays, and maintenance is handled through local contractors so nobody is waiting on you to drive up. You will get a Slack channel for day-to-day contact, a monthly statement between the 1st and 5th, and a quarterly call. Distance genuinely is not the problem here — the properties in our portfolio with absent owners perform the same as the local ones.
What compliance do I legally need in place?+
For short lets in this part of Cumbria there is currently no strict formal registration regime, but that is not the same as nothing to do. The practical baseline is working smoke alarms, a carbon monoxide alarm, fire blankets and extinguishers, a fire risk assessment, gas and electrical safety certificates, and insurance that explicitly permits short-term letting — a standard residential policy will not cover you. On fire doors: I suggested deferring those until around six months in, purely to keep your upfront spend down while you see how the property performs. That is a cost-sequencing suggestion, not a legal opinion — if your fire risk assessment says you need them, you need them.
Will a mortgage lender accept these income figures?+
We can provide the income projection in a format you can hand to a lender or broker, and we do this regularly. Be aware that most lenders will not underwrite against projected short-let income for a first-time holiday let purchase — they typically assess affordability on your own income, or on a long-let rental valuation, which here is around £8,942 a year. That is worth knowing before you build a plan around it. Speak to a broker who specialises in holiday-let mortgages early; it will shape what you can offer on the property.
What is the honest worst case if the projections are not hit?+
The realistic downside is not zero bookings — it is a slower ramp than modelled. Every comparable in your area is an established listing averaging 96 reviews and a 5.0 rating. Yours starts at zero. Listings typically become competitive at around 10 strong reviews, and reaching the local average takes 11 to 16 months. That is why Year 1 is modelled at 80% of the full run rate: £17,578 net rather than £21,972. If the ramp is slower than that — a poor first summer, a weak review start — you could reasonably land in the £14,000–£16,000 range in Year 1. Even at the low end, that is still well above the £8,942 the property would earn as a long let. The genuine worst case is a property that does not suit short letting at all: too remote for guests, or a season that collapses. I do not think that applies here given 12 active comparables all holding 5.0 ratings, but you should hold the risk in mind rather than have me talk you out of it.
How much will it cost to furnish and set up?+
For a three-bedroom property furnished from empty, a full Stayful setup comes in at around £6,590 — that covers beds, soft furnishings, kitchen and electrical packs, paint, dining and living furniture, professional photography, labour and key safes. That figure is indicative and based on our standard three-bed specification; once you have completed and I can see the property, I will send you a proper itemised quote. On the projected Year 1 income, that cost is recovered around month six of trading.
