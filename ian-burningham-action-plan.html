What actually drives bookings in Cambridge

🎓

The University

Thirty-one colleges, graduation ceremonies, December admissions interviews, open days and a constant conference calendar. It produces visiting parents, academics and delegates all year, with sharp predictable spikes we can price into.

🔬

Addenbrooke's & the Biomedical Campus

One of the largest concentrations of hospital and medical research activity in Europe, sitting a couple of miles from you. Patient families, visiting clinicians and campus contractors book longer stays and — importantly — they book in January and February too.

💼

Silicon Fen

Cambridge Science Park and the surrounding tech and life-sciences cluster generate steady midweek corporate and project stays. That's the demand that fills the gaps tourism leaves behind.

🚆

Fifty minutes to King's Cross

Cambridge station is walkable from Argyle Street. That's a genuine selling point for guests — and it's the same reason the flat works for you when you're back and commuting into London.

🛶

Tourism, April to September

The colleges, punting on the Cam, the Fitzwilliam. This is concentrated and seasonal, and it's exactly where the £4,207 July figure comes from.

🍜

Mill Road and Romsey

Independent restaurants, cafés and shops on the doorstep, and a residential street rather than a block of identical serviced flats. It reads differently to guests scrolling a listing page, and it's worth building the photography around.

On your saturation question — "is there enough demand for another one?" Honestly, that's the wrong question and I said as much on the call. Cambridge has the demand; it's one of the strongest short-let markets in the country alongside Oxford, Bath, Bristol and London. The real question is how long it takes to climb past listings that already hold 4.8 ratings and 135 reviews. That's a ranking problem, not a demand problem, and the answer is 11–17 months of doing the unglamorous work properly.
