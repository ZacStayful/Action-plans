2. The lending question (your main concern)

  • Nothing stops short-letting a Grade II listed building. The hurdle is the lender, because not every lender will lend against a listed building for holiday-let use.
  • If you want a restaurant tenant on the ground floor with two short-let flats above, a commercial mortgage is the route to explore first. Buy-to-let lenders are rigid on this kind of mixed use.
  • A solicitor and a good mortgage broker will help most with the listing and lending details. We can supply the income figures but cannot give lender advice.
  • Run as a business, the property may add value compared with room-by-room letting, but that is not guaranteed.

Your questions

Can I short-let a Grade II listed building?

Nothing stops you short-letting it. The difficulty is finding a lender who will lend against a listed building for short-let use. Check with a solicitor and broker before you commit.

What if I can't resell it easily?

That is a fair concern: fewer buyers can get lending on listed buildings. Running it as a business may add value, but that isn't guaranteed. Take legal and mortgage advice.

What do I actually have to do?

Pay the mortgage and bills, plus utilities and WiFi. We handle everything else, including full remote management as you live in Newcastle.

What does it cost?

15% plus VAT of booking value. No bookings, no fee. Airbnb and Booking.com fees and cleaning are additional.

Smart lock or key safe?

We'd go with a key safe per flat: cheaper, and fewer things go wrong, like flat batteries.

What is the honest worst case if projections are not hit?

Income could be lower, especially in the first months while reviews build and in January and February. The figures are indicative, not guaranteed. Your mortgage and bills still have to be paid, so stress-test your numbers with your lender before committing.
