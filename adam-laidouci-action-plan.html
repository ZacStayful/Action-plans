Your questions, answered honestly

Nobody can guarantee bookings, and Year 1 is the slowest year because a new listing has to earn its first reviews. Your comparable listings average 73 reviews each and are already established, so we plan for Year 1 at 80% of the full figure (£43,473 net, not £54,341). Here is what the same model gives in tougher cases, using the Property Analysis numbers:

  • Your 40% occupancy scenario (same nightly rate): about £38,815 a year at full run rate, or about £31,052 in Year 1.
  • Half of the projection: about £27,170 a year.
  • Long-let comparison: £17,269 net a year. On this model, short-let only drops below that if occupancy falls to roughly 17.8% at the same nightly rate.

All of these figures are before your mortgage, bills, council tax and insurance. They also assume the standard guest mix, and your families and corporate contractors preference may narrow it. That effect cannot be measured in advance, so none of the figures above include it. These are our own arithmetic on the analysis, not a second forecast.

Every booking goes through ID checks and a security deposit. We run a two-night minimum, a minimum price floor (you can set it, or we will), and a strict no-parties rule. Cheap, short stays are where most problems start, and those rules remove a lot of them.

We will put families and corporate contractors only, no under-25s on the listing and build it into how we screen. To be straight with you: that is a front-door filter, not a guarantee. A guest can misrepresent who they are, and we cannot give you a platform-level exclusion list. That is why we recommend the owner-side backup in the next answer. Your street is residential and not near a nightlife area, which helps.

We recommend three things you control, all feeding straight into the shared messaging channel with our team:

  • Doorbell cameras at the front and back, so you can see arrivals and activity.
  • A decibel sensor in the living room that alerts you if noise stays above a set level.
  • WiFi device alerts: if 15 devices connect to a house that sleeps 10, that is a flag.

If an alert fires, message us in the channel and we act on it straight away. Alerts caught early are usually easy to resolve. Cameras and noise sensors must be disclosed on the listing. Cameras must only cover outside areas, and noise sensors must not record audio.

There are three layers. First, the guest's security deposit. Extra deep-cleaning after a trashed stay is claimed from that deposit, not from you. Second, Airbnb AirCover, which is the main protection for serious damage. We manage the claim, so you never deal with Airbnb directly. In roughly seven years of operating, Zac has only needed to call on AirCover twice, and it works much like an insurance claim. Airbnb moves the guests, gives you time to repair, and payment can take two to three months to arrive.

The honest limit: we are not aware of a specialist short-let insurance policy for this, and Stayful's liability for guest damage is limited. A very large loss outside AirCover's terms would sit with you. Two things worth checking with your own advisers before you start: whether your mortgage lender and home insurer allow short-let use. We are not insurance or lending advisers.

The analysis uses 56% occupancy at an average of £509 a night. Across the 12 comparable listings within 13.24 km, the average is 55% at £511. Short-let occupancy runs higher than hotels because one booking takes the whole house off the market for those nights, and Airbnb's average is often nearer 70%. Yours is lower because it is a four-bedroom at a high nightly rate, and that trade-off means less wear and tear.

If you are right and it is closer to 40% at the same rate, net would be about £38,815 a year, roughly £3,235 a month. That is still about 2.2x the long-let figure, before your mortgage and bills. The comparison listings are established, with many reviews, and ours starts with none. That is why Year 1 is shown at 80%.

We handle up to £250 of maintenance spend without asking you. Once the month's spend passes that, we tell you in the messaging channel. Anything urgent that interrupts a guest is actioned straight away, whatever the time difference. Minor jobs are batched with other properties nearby on a day rate, so they cost less.

For a big job such as a boiler, we notify you with the price. You can ask us to go ahead, or shop around yourself. We add no markup to contractor invoices. You pay what the tradesperson charges.

You get one PDF statement on the 1st of each month for the stays that ended the month before. One payout follows to the bank account in your agreement, between the 1st and 5th. You can see all previous statements and keep your own record.

As you will be overseas, HMRC may treat you as a non-resident landlord. That can mean tax withheld at 20% from rental income unless you complete HMRC's opt-out paperwork. You told us you will complete that form so we can pay you gross, and handle your own tax return with your accountant. That works for us, and our statements give your accountant what they need. We are not tax advisers, so please confirm the detail with your accountant.

We operate about 15 hours a day, every day, covering guests and owners. Most issues do not happen out of hours, but if one does you can message the channel and we act. You do not need a local friend or relative as an emergency contact. We regularly look after properties for owners who live overseas.
