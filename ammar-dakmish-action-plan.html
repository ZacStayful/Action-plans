What the 90-night rule actually means for you

The rule, correctly

In London, letting a home as short-term "temporary sleeping accommodation" is lawful without planning permission for up to 90 nights per calendar year, as long as council tax is paid on the property. Stays of 90+ consecutive nights by the same guest don't count. Stays of 28–89 nights do. The count belongs to the house, not the listing or the manager, and resets on 1 January. More than 90 nights means applying to Kingston Council for planning permission (change of use). There is no licence to apply for.
