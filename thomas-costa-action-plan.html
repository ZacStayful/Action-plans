Your questions, answered honestly

Would the worst month be fully in our pocket, or are there deductions? +
The figures we showed you are already net — after online platform commission, Stayful's management fee, and cleaning & laundry. What's on the page is what lands in your account.
Who's responsible for the keys while we're in Switzerland? +
Either a key safe with rotating codes, or a Keynest local key-storage service with 24-hour access if there's nowhere to fit a key safe. Either way, you're not needed on the ground.
What happens if a guest damages the furniture or appliances you're leaving behind? +
Every guest pays a £200–£400 deposit, and we run ID checks and host-history checks on every booking. Minor damage comes out of that deposit. Anything major is covered by insurance. If it's fair wear and tear rather than guest-caused, we coordinate the fix and it's deducted from your statement — but only after we flag it to you (our threshold for owner sign-off is £300/month).
We're still on a residential mortgage and subletting — will that cause a problem? +
Zac's honest recommendation: launch as-is for 3–6 months rather than switching your mortgage straight away. That way, if short-let isn't for you, you haven't done a lot of admin for nothing. If you do stay on it long-term, switching to a buy-to-let or holiday-let mortgage typically roughly halves the mortgage cost (interest-only vs. principal + interest) and keeps things fully compliant.
Can we block dates if we want to use the flat ourselves? +
Yes — no fee. You block dates directly in your live calendar. We'd recommend 30–60 days' notice where possible so it doesn't clash with a confirmed booking.
What's the honest worst case if these projections aren't hit? +
In a genuinely quiet month — most likely Jan–Mar before reviews build — net could sit close to your ~£1,400/month overheads, meaning a small loss or break-even rather than a guaranteed profit. That's the real downside case, and it's exactly why we've shown you the worst months, not just the average.
