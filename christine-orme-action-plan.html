Having checked: the Furniture and Furnishings (Fire) (Safety) Regulations 1988 do apply to furniture supplied in the course of a business, and short-term letting is caught by that. Sofas, mattresses, headboards, cushions and upholstered chairs need the permanent fire-safety label attached. In practice most furniture bought new in the UK since 1990 already carries one, so this may cost you nothing at all — but your ornate metal-framed bed and any older or second-hand upholstered pieces need checking before we go live. It's beneficial, but it's slower and more conditional than came across on the call, so here is the accurate version. Once you short-let, the property is treated as a second home for council tax and North Kesteven can charge a premium on top — you mentioned your bill jumping to around £264, which matches. To move onto business rates instead you apply to the Valuation Office Agency, but in England the property must have been available to let for at least 140 nights and actually let for at least 70 nights in the previous 12 months. That means you cannot apply from day one — you need a full letting year behind you first. If you meet the thresholds and the rateable value is low enough, Small Business Rates Relief will very likely take the bill to zero, which is a meaningful saving. But budget for paying the council tax premium through your first year, not from the start.

Zac told you on the call he wasn't certain, and that was the honest answer — it still is, and this is a question for a UK accountant rather than a letting agent. What's worth putting in front of them: the Furnished Holiday Lettings tax regime, which used to give short lets favourable capital gains treatment, was abolished from April 2025, so any advice you read online from before then is out of date.
